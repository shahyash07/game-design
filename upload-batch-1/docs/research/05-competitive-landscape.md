# StaySmart competitive brief

Research brief, 24 October 2026. Persona: competitive intelligence analyst. Research only.

## 1. Landscape: daily brain and puzzle products

**LinkedIn Games (8 titles, one puzzle per day, resets midnight PT)**
- Lineup: Pinpoint, Crossclimb, Queens (Apr 30 2024); Tango (Oct 7 2024); Zip (Mar 18 2025); Mini Sudoku (Aug 12 2025); Patches (Mar 18/19 2026, Shikaku-style rectangle fill, in development >1 year); Wend (Jun 2026, Wordle-meets-word-search, first new word game in two years). LinkedIn says it is not accepting outside game submissions.
- Stats (company-reported): "millions" of daily players, "billions of puzzles solved"; 86% return the next day, 82% still playing after seven days (Wend launch coverage); earlier figure 84%. Third-party estimate ~3.5M daily players (May 2026, unverified).
- Mechanics: streak = count of daily wins with milestones; a streak freeze after five straight wins (third-party claim); a Share button after every puzzle posts score or leaderboard to your network ("scores only reach your network if you choose"); connections leaderboard plus company and school leaderboards (2025).
- Premium: no tie-in found. No official archive or unlimited mode; only fan-run archives exist. Games are a free engagement and distribution play, not a paid product.

**NYT Games**
- 2024 volume (reported Jun 2025): 11.1B plays; Wordle 5.3B, Connections 3.3B, Strands 1.3B; 10M+ daily players; Games had 1M+ standalone subscribers as of 2023. Company total 13.08M subs (Q1 2026), 13.35M (Jun 2026), target 15M by end-2027. Q2 2026 digital ad revenue +21%, management credits engagement in news, games and sports.
- 2025-26 moves: Pips (logic, free) launched Aug 18 2025; Mini, Tiles and Letter Boxed paywalled Aug 2025; Spelling Bee free only to "Solid"; Strands archive sub-only Sept 2025; Crossplay (multiplayer) US launch Jan 21 2026; Midi Crossword Feb 2026; Connections: Sports Edition (Feb 2025) with a soccer edition now daily; chess beta "Capture" discontinued Feb 27 2026. Free core remains Wordle, Connections, Strands, Pips.
- Growth engine is the shareable emoji grid, not streaks: 1.2M Wordle shares on Twitter in 13 days (Jan 2022), ~140K TikTok videos.

**Brain-training subscription apps**
- Lumosity: claims 100M+ registered (unverified), ~$70M revenue (secondary source); LPI lifetime index; 3 free games/day; 2016 FTC $2M settlement. Pivoted toward medicine: LumosityRx got FDA 510(k) clearance Dec 10 2025 as a prescription digital therapeutic for adult ADHD (13 games, n>500 trial).
- Elevate: EPQ score 0-5000, six levels Novice to Master, five skill areas, compared with age peers; ~$1M/month iOS (dated Sensor Tower estimate).
- Peak: 40+ games, "Coach" plans, Brain Score. Impulse (GMRD): 4.65 stars from ~1.4M reviews, IQ/personality/EQ tests, streaks, ~$1M/month; dominant complaint is aggressive monetisation and hard cancellation.
- Brilliant: 10M+ learners, daily problems, two free "keys" a day, ~$150/yr. NeuroNation: adaptive difficulty, added AI feedback reports with offline exercise suggestions in 2025. CogniFit: assessment-first (23 skills), daily 0-100 cognitive score and "Brain Age", $19.99/mo.
- Duolingo: 58.7M DAU Q2 2026 (+23%); Chess ~7M DAU within a year, Math and Music only single-digit millions each; CEO now says Math should target K-12, not adults. Real-time multiplayer modes added 2026. Lesson: adult mental-math as a subject does not scale on its own.

**Mental-math apps**: commoditised and tiny. Google Play bands: Hiza "Mental arithmetic" 1M+ (4.6 stars), "Mental Math: Everyday Speed" 1M+ (500 word problems), Mental Math Master 100K+ (3.0 stars), Quick Math (Shiny Things, handwriting input, grades 2-6), "Quick Math: Brain Trainer" with a GRE/GMAT quant mode, open-source Mental Math on F-Droid, Numbio (iOS, 8K ratings). None has a social loop or a state-of-mind framing.

**Rings / readiness metaphors**: no brain app uses Apple-style rings or a readiness score. Closest: a six-domain daily puzzle set yielding a 0-100 Daily Cognitive Score, and CogniFit's daily score. All mainstream brain apps score a lifetime level (LPI, EPQ), never "how you are today". The readiness metaphor lives entirely in wearables.

## 2. Readiness checks and pre-decision rituals

**Direct precedents (all small, none with content)**
- CogniTrack (free, iOS/Android): PVT reaction test; 10-trial baseline taken "when you feel your best", 6-trial quick check under a minute; four-level verdict vs your own baseline: Superb / Good / Sluggish ("consider rest") / Impaired ("avoid demanding tasks"); 7/30/90-day trends; on-device data. This is the closest existing "am I sharp?" product.
- MentalMather (iOS, 2 ratings): daily timed-arithmetic "Sharpness Test" producing a Sharpness Score vs personal baseline, framed as "cognitive throughput, not math skill". MindBenchmark Daily: one Speed/Memory/Focus test per day with an entertainment disclaimer.
- NeuroTracker: vendor says coaches assess daily cognitive readiness as deviation from baseline (score = tracking speed at 50% success); offers a free 6-minute test. BlazePod, FitLight, Reflexion, Sportreact log session reaction data but have no baseline-deviation readiness check; evidence base is small (6-week youth study, Nov 2025).

**Trading psychology** (the only space with a pre-decision ritual): TradeMind (iOS) has a daily pledge before the open, a readiness score and a "Tilt Protocol" (name your state, then push through or stop). Plancana logs mood before and after each trade and reports "N losing trades followed mood X". Edgewonk's Tiltmeter rates emotion per trade and correlates with P&L and deviation from baseline. TradingPal adds a mid-day check-in and Apple Watch stress alerts. TraderSync/Tradervue have only basic tags.

**Wearable readiness scores: presentation**
- Oura Readiness: 0-100; 85+ "ready for action", 70-84 "recovered well enough", <70 "pay attention".
- Whoop Recovery: 0-100%, green 67-100 / yellow 34-66 / red 0-33, paired with Strain; explicitly a "verdict".
- Garmin Body Battery: continuous 0-100 charge that drains through the day and recharges with rest.

**Why users love them**: one number, a colour, a trend over 60-90 days, coach-friendly data, a habit nudge.
**Why they distrust them**: composites are unvalidated (2025 review of 14 wearable scores found few validated; no head-to-head; formulas unpublished; Lundstrom 2024: raw HRV carried the signal, the composite did not); Garmin HRV trails Oura/Whoop (CCC 0.87 vs 0.94-0.99); unexplained algorithm shifts (Fenix 8, Apr 2025); "output without insight"; a bad score "sets the tone for the day"; orthosomnia prevalence 3-14% (n=523, 2024). Sleep doctors' advice: if the score says bad but you feel fine, trust how you feel. Design lesson: never ship a verdict without a plan and a trend, and let the user's self-report sit beside the number.

## 3. AI-judgment and critical-thinking training

- Inoculation games: Bad News (one 15-minute play cut perceived reliability of fake news by 21%, effect holds ~3 months with "booster" replays); Go Viral! (5 minutes, raised perceived manipulativeness and cut sharing intent, EN/DE/FR); Cranky Uncle (cartoon mentor; climate and vaccine editions; reduced vaccine hesitancy; Arabic RCT 2026). 2025 caveat: players become more likely to call everything fake (a "cry wolf" bias). None publishes retention; they are one-shot academic interventions with no daily loop.
- Calibration: Clearer Thinking's "Calibrate Your Judgment" (Open Philanthropy-funded; thousands of questions, per-answer score, calibration curve); Metaculus calls it the best free calibration training. Scoring can be gamed (creator acknowledged). Quantified Intuitions (confidence-interval trivia, pastcasting) runs ~90 visitors/month; Fatebook ~50K forecasts. Overconfidence Analyzer reached 6,000+ users after an NYT feature. Niche, loved, unmonetised.
- Real-or-AI: Fake-or-Real (8,000+ players, daily challenge), "AI or Real?" (Play, deepfake video mode), "Real or AI: Photo Quiz" (8 seconds per card, each card explains the giveaway), WhichOneIsReal (daily + 1v1), Northwestern Detect Fakes, Reality Defender's 30-item test, Minecraft "AI Detective". No consumer app with verified millions.
- Google: Be Internet Awesome AI-literacy curriculum (Jun 2025, grades 2-8, "understanding and critical thinking about AI, not operating tools"); AI Quests (Sept 9 2025, with Stanford, ages 11-14, browser, no login, data-cleaning and model-training quests); Gemini training for 6M US educators. No reach figures published.
- Spot-the-hallucination: only an open-source party game (35-claim bank, 2-8 players), an interactive blog guide and a free micro-course (updated Jun 2026). No commercial product.
- Stanford Civic Online Reasoning: lateral reading; RCT showed a modest time investment helps; baseline problem is severe (52% of 3,446 students accepted a grainy Russian video as "strong evidence").
- Workplace pull: EU AI Act Art. 4 literacy duty since Feb 2025; US DoL AI Literacy Framework (Feb 2026) names "evaluating outputs" as a core area; Cornerstone 2026 survey: 46% of AI-using employees have no formal training. L&D vendors now propose "share of AI outputs verified" as the KPI, yet no consumer product trains it daily.

**What works**: 5-15 minute single plays with a booster cadence; immediate feedback that explains the giveaway; a personal calibration curve; a character or narrative. **What fails**: no retention loop (academic games), no mass-market calibration product, and "spot the fake" skills that decay into blanket scepticism.

## 4. Gaps and white space

- Nobody scores "how sharp are you today". LinkedIn and NYT measure streaks and solve time; Lumosity/Elevate give a lifetime index; wearables infer readiness passively from the body, with contested maths and no plan attached. Active, 2-minute, self-referenced cognitive readiness with an action attached is empty except for CogniTrack and a two-rating indie app.
- Nobody trains AI-output checking at a daily consumer cadence; everything is K-12, academic or corporate course-ware.
- Nobody gives feedback on written reasoning (NeuroNation's AI reports are the closest).
- Nobody in the category uses audio.
- Your eight LinkedIn clones are parity, not differentiation: LinkedIn has 86% D1 return and network distribution for free. Win on the layer LinkedIn does not have (diagnosis, judgment, AI-checking), and keep readiness claims strictly "relative to your own baseline" to avoid Lumosity's 2016 FTC fate.

**Positioning**: StaySmart is the daily mental gym for professionals who work alongside AI: ten minutes of puzzles that tell you, against your own baseline, whether you are sharp enough to make today's call yourself. LinkedIn and NYT reward streaks and wearables score your body; StaySmart scores your judgment and trains the one habit AI erodes, thinking first.

**Five differentiators the competition lacks**
1. Mind Readiness Check: three active rounds vs personal baseline, a three-band verdict (borrow Oura's bands and Whoop's colours) that always ships with a next action ("decide now" / "re-run in 20 minutes" / "defer, draft with AI, decide later") plus a 30-day trend and a self-report slider, fixing the "verdict without a plan" complaint.
2. Daily AI-output-checking tile: spot the hallucination, find the unsupported step, pick the right answer among plausible AI drafts, each card explaining the giveaway and penalising blanket scepticism (counters the "cry wolf" effect).
3. AI feedback on a 60-second written-reasoning round, scored on structure, missing evidence and stated confidence vs correctness (Clearer Thinking's calibration curve brought to a mass audience).
4. A standing calibration/overconfidence metric beside speed and accuracy ("am I overconfident today?"), something no puzzle or brain app tracks.
5. Short info-dense audio explainers on the cognitive trap behind each puzzle, usable on a commute, where no competitor has audio at all.
